# Content

## Guides

A guide earns its page by answering something a real person searches for, better than what is already there.

1. **Pick the need.** Search it first and read what ranks. If the results are all giants or the need is thin, pick
   another. Prefer specific ("calm abc game for toddlers") over broad ("abc game").
2. **Write only what you can prove.** Rules, prices and features come from the listing, the README or the app's docs,
   read that day. Each guide should be genuinely useful to someone who never installs the app.
3. **Check.** Title up to 60 characters, description up to 170, at least 150 words, `updated` set. The site's `check`
   enforces these and Apple's wording rules (say "the App Store"; do not name iOS).
4. **Link.** Link related guides. The page adds the App Store badge itself.

Not doorway pages: never publish near-copies with a keyword swapped, and never use another product's name to catch its
searches. One good guide a week per app beats ten thin ones, and thin pages can sink a whole site.

## Voice

Plain, specific and short. Say what the thing does. No "ultimate", "best", "revolutionary" or "#1", and no ranking claim
without a source. Read the app's `rules` first: some words are banned per app.

## Short video

`app-shorts` owns the craft. Before posting, a reviewer who did not make the cut watches the hook and payoff frames and
checks each caption against what the take shows. That reviewer replaces the owner's watch in unattended runs. A cut that
does not pass is logged `deferred`, not posted.
