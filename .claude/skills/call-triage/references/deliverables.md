# The page and the email

## When to make a page

When the person is owed something with parts (a summary, instructions, a link), or when it would save them time. Skip it
when a three-line email would do. The owner sends it, so it speaks in the owner's voice.

## The page

Lead with what they need to act on. A usual order: thanks, how to try the thing, what we heard, what we're doing about it,
what we'd like to ask them.

- Host it on the product's own site, in its design, as an unlisted page: a random path segment, `noindex`, out of the
  sitemap and the navigation. Unlisted is not private, so write it as if anyone might read it.
- Do not name the person. "Our call" is enough, and they know who they are. No quotes, and nothing about their family,
  employer, clients or students.
- **What we heard must be something they'd recognise and agree with.** Offer it for correction.
- State the true status of each idea: built, planned, or just being considered. A suggestion is not a promise.
- Give the honest limits of the product, as the product's own copy rules require.
- Ask few questions, and only ones whose answers would change what gets built.
- Test every link and step in the instructions, and say plainly when one isn't ready.

Run `guard --file` on the page's source before deploying, and read the live page afterwards.

## The email

- Write it to `email-draft.md` in the call folder and, when a mail connector is available, also as a draft in the account
  the conversation was held from. Check that the connected account is the right one: the thread may live in another
  mailbox, and a draft from the wrong address is worse than a file.
- Never call a send tool. Never fill in an address you have not seen in the owner's mail or been given.
- Keep it short and plain, in the owner's voice. It carries the link and the one thing to do next.
- If the page depends on something not ready yet, say when the email can go.
- Leave the consent line to the owner when the recording wasn't mentioned on the call: include it, and flag it in the
  report.
