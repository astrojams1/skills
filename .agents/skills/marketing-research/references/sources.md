# Where to look, and how to grade it

The conference never ends, and no list of speakers is complete. Use these as the first places to look each week.
Add a source when it teaches something strong. Mark one weak when its claims keep failing to hold up.

## Grades

| Grade | What it is | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | A first-hand result with numbers and a method: a paper or preprint, a platform's own data or rules, or the team that did the work, named, with a before and after | Becomes an idea for a business; can change a skill |
| **B** | A named practitioner with a track record describing their own work, with numbers but little method; a vendor's own customer story | Becomes an idea for a business, marked provisional |
| **C** | Secondhand claims, launch posts, listicles, anonymous threads, predictions, "10x" with no number behind it | Raises a question for next week; never an idea on its own |

Raise a grade one step when two independent sources report the same result. Lower it one step when the source sells
the thing it praises and says nothing about what failed.

## Fit

Grade fit as well. Our businesses are small, have no ad budget, and are run by agents with no person watching. A
finding fits best when it works with:

- **No spending and no new accounts.** A tactic that needs paid ads or a sales team goes on the owner's options line.
- **Agents running unattended,** on a schedule, judged on their own numbers.
- **Our channels:** search and AI answers (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity), email under each sender's rules,
  YouTube Shorts, free web tools and data pages, directories, partners, the App Store, Patreon.
- **Our audiences:** people who download small apps, Las Vegas small businesses, 18 to 29 year olds in Las Vegas,
  Clean Streets patrons, and school speech-language pathologists.

## Kinds of source

Look in at least four kinds each week.

1. **Research.** arXiv (cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.IR, cs.HC) on LLM agents in marketing, persuasion, personalization, and
   generative engine optimization (being cited in AI answers; start from Aggarwal et al., "GEO", KDD 2024). SSRN and
   NBER marketing papers. Harvard Business Review, MIT Sloan Management Review, Wharton's AI research.
2. **Platforms and model makers.** What Anthropic, OpenAI and Google publish on agents and on how their AI answers
   pick sources; Google Search Central; YouTube's Creator Insider and help pages; Apple's App Store news; Meta,
   HubSpot, Salesforce, Shopify, Klaviyo and Intercom on their marketing agents. Their customer stories are B.
3. **Practitioners with a record.** Lenny Rachitsky (newsletter and podcast), Kyle Poyar (Growth Unhinged), Elena
   Verna, Andrew Chen, Emily Kramer (MKT1), Rand Fishkin (SparkToro), Kieran Flanagan, Reforge, Ethan Mollick (One
   Useful Thing), Simon Willison on agents, Paul Roetzer (Marketing AI Institute), Kevin Indig (Growth Memo), Aleyda
   Solis, Lily Ray and Mike King (iPullRank) on AI search.
4. **Conferences and talks.** Recorded talks and write-ups from MAICON, AI Engineer World's Fair, SaaStr, INBOUND,
   MarTech, BrightonSEO, Web Summit and Lenny's events. Read the slides or transcript, not the hype post about it.
5. **Case studies from builders.** Indie Hackers, Hacker News, founders' own posts with real numbers, Product Hunt
   launch retrospectives, app developers' ASO write-ups. Usually B or C; follow each to its numbers.
6. **Industry data.** Similarweb, Sensor Tower, Appfigures, Pew Research, and trade groups for our audiences
   (for speech pathologists, ASHA). Platform-wide numbers, graded by method.
